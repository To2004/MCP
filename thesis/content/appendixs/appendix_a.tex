This appendix walks a single call through the full McpRisk pipeline of
\Cref{chapter:framework}, to make the interaction of the static and parameter
layers concrete.

\section*{The scenario}

An agent connected to the corporate-filesystem server issues:
\begin{quote}
\texttt{move\_file(src="/sensitive/security/private\_key.pem",
dst="/public/shared/key.pem")}
\end{quote}

\section*{Static cell score}

The scanner has already classified the tool and the asset at design time:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Asset sensitivity} $s=5$. The target \texttt{private\_key.pem}
        is a regulated / crown-jewel secret (\Cref{tab:sensitivity}).
  \item \textbf{Tool impact} $i=3$. \texttt{move\_file} maps to the atomic
        operation \textsc{move}, but because it removes the file from its
        protected location it is treated as access-changing and irreversible at
        the top of its impact class.
  \item \textbf{Blast radius} $b=4$. Relocating a key from a restricted folder
        to a public one exposes it system-wide.
\end{itemize}

Applying \Cref{eq:static}, the raw score is
$\text{score}=s\cdot b\cdot i = 5\cdot 4\cdot 3 = 60$, the maximum on the scale.
By the calibration of \Cref{tab:bands} the cell satisfies the \textit{critical}
condition ($i=3 \wedge s=5 \wedge b\ge 3$), so the inherent cell band is
\textbf{critical}.

\section*{Parameter escalation}

The parameter rubric for \texttt{move\_file} flags the destination path as a
magnitude-bearing argument, with a higher \texttt{base\_rank} when the
destination is world-readable. Here the destination \texttt{/public/shared/}
lands in the top value band. Combining the two ranks with \Cref{eq:paramavg}
gives a parameter risk of \textit{critical}, and the escalation of
\Cref{eq:escalate} takes
$\text{final\_band}=\max(\textit{critical},\textit{critical})=\textbf{critical}$.
The call is therefore surfaced at the top band --- and indeed calls of exactly
this shape (\texttt{move\_file} on a crown-jewel asset, raw score $60$) are the
highest-ranked entries in the captured-call corpus of
\Cref{sec:experiments:results:dynamic}.

\section*{Contrast: a benign call}

For comparison, \texttt{read\_file("/public/README.md")} scores $s=1$, $i=1$,
$b=1$, giving a raw score of $1$ and a band of \textbf{low} --- it is neither
gated nor escalated. This contrast illustrates the design goal of
\Cref{sec:framework:model}: ordinary work stays low while the destruction or
exfiltration of a sensitive asset rises to the top of the pyramid.
